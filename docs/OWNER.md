# The owner: Tiago Gil

Who this site is for and about, and how to work with Tiago. Read this before writing copy, proposing
design, or starting any larger task. Facts about roles, dates and projects live in `src/content/`; this file holds
what isn't written anywhere else.

---

## 1. Positioning

- **Frontend developer first.** Works mainly with React, Next.js and TypeScript. Currently at Aquasis (Lisbon) on
  Aquaworks. Has also worked full-stack (Subvisual, Talent Protocol) and is comfortable with databases, APIs and the
  backend when a project needs it; the headline stays frontend.
- **Architecture is the background, not the pitch.** Master's in Architecture (FAUL), a year as an architect at CR
  Espassos, then the switch to software in 2023 (Ironhack). It shows in how Tiago works (structure,

  it's a fact and in one line of the hero and About intros.
- **Interest in UX and UI.** Said plainly ("an interest in UX and UI"), never "enthusiast" or "keen".
- **Available for freelance**, not "open to work": Tiago is employed. Remove the freelance lines when booked.
- **Games thread.** The master's thesis was on what architecture can learn from video games; several personal
  projects are game companions (Voydex, House of Legends). The rest are mostly small trackers.

## 2. Voice

- Plain, human, the words people actually use. Short sentences, one idea each.
- Facts and habits, not claims: "I plan components before building", not "products grow without breaking".
- Nothing that isn't in the content or confirmed by Tiago. When unsure, ask.
- When copy keeps missing, ask Tiago for a few rough lines in Tiago's own words and polish those. The hero only
  worked once it started from Tiago's own draft.

**Approved** (use as the reference for tone):
- Hero: "Frontend developer / & architect." + "I work mainly with React, Next.js and TypeScript. My background in
  architecture makes me care a lot about structure and design. Currently at Aquasis, in Lisbon, and available for
  freelance."
- About intro: "A frontend developer with a background in architecture and an interest in UX and UI."
- About bio: "I'm based in Lisbon and have been building for the web since 2023, mostly with React, Next.js and
  TypeScript. / Before that I was an architect, and I wrote my master's thesis on architecture and video games.
  Games stuck with me: several of my own projects are game companions, like Voydex, a Pokémon companion I'm building
  now. The rest are mostly small trackers for things I care about, from concerts to finances."
- How I work: "I pay attention to hierarchy, spacing and states, so screens are easy to read." (habits, not promises)
- Footer: "Frontend developer and former architect, based in Lisbon."
- Scope points (in the study's `SCOPE` table): a short title, then one full factual sentence.

**Rejected** (don't bring back):
- "Frontend developer. / Interfaces built to last." (slogan), "I'm Tiago" as the headline.
- "…building clear web interfaces that last", "web products", "long-term maintainability", "systems, constraints",
  "sits between frontend engineering and product design", "operational interfaces", "builder-reputation products".
- "keen interest", "enthusiast", "Practice" for the CR Espassos job, "First job", "Background: Architecture
  2014–2023 · Software since 2023" (hard to read).
- Ambitious principles ("so interfaces feel obvious", "products grow without breaking", "works for everyone").
- "bootcamp apps" for the Learning projects.
- Bios that only restate what the page already shows (role, stack, Aquasis, architecture).

## 3. Language rules

- English and European Portuguese (pt-PT), always both.
- Role names never translated: Frontend Developer, Full-Stack Developer (see `docs/CONTENT.md` §2 for spelling).
- Portuguese dates: "jan 2025", never "01/2025".
- Avoid em dashes in UI text (still an open question; default to commas).

## 4. Design taste

- Calm, crafted, modern; nothing generic or templated. Both the old site and a Claude Design attempt were rejected
  as too generic.
- **Quick to scan** is the top test: few sections, short entries, nothing repeated, no decorative labels.
- Sections must read as distinct areas (background bands). Loves ruled, tabular, information-dense parts with dates
  on the left (the Timeline chart, "Where I've worked").
- Restrained motion: subtle, once, reduced-motion aware. Over-animation reads as gimmicky.
- Notices spacing and alignment closely: hover plates must not touch text; things in a row must line up; padding
  that's too big gets noticed.
- Large text only for short intros; long text at reading size (big fonts across long text feel "massive").
- No boxes for the sake of boxes, no lines that don't separate rows, no full-width rules.

## 5. How Tiago likes to work

- **Answer the questions first.** When a message asks questions, answer all of them before acting, committing or
  moving on. Don't bury answers under work.
- **Candid verdicts.** Give an honest opinion ("it's good enough" when it is) and one recommendation, not a menu.
  Offer two or three options only when the direction is genuinely open.
- **One version.** Avoid switches, presets and matrices of choices.
- **Mockups first** for visual changes: try them in the study (`docs/study/`), log the decision in
  `docs/DECISIONS.md`, then build.
- **Show, don't describe:** after a visible change, share a screenshot.
- **Commits:** title line only (conventional style). No body, no co-author or attribution lines. Never push, merge
  or deploy without asking.
- **Plain language** in replies too: no jargon, no internal names without explaining them.
